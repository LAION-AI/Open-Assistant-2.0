## 1. Purpose and expected benefit

What the work is for, and who benefits. Say plainly whether participants
themselves benefit — often they do not, which raises the bar for voluntariness.

## 2. Participants

Who they are, how they are recruited, whether any group is vulnerable, whether
anything pressures them to take part, and what they lose by declining.

## 3. Data collected

Every category, including anything free-text that could contain more than
intended. State explicitly whether Art. 9 GDPR special-category data is a target
of collection, and whether it can be excluded in practice.

## 4. Consent

How consent is obtained, what exactly it covers, how it is recorded and
versioned, how it is withdrawn, and what withdrawal cannot undo. Consent to
publication must be separate from acceptance of terms of use.

## 5. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation | Residual |
|---|---|---|

Name the residual risk honestly. A protocol whose residual column is empty has
not been thought about.

## 6. Data minimisation, retention, security

What is kept, for how long, where, under whose control, and how participants
remove it.

## 7. What the committee is being asked to decide

Specific questions, not "please approve". Point at the parts you are unsure
about — that is what the review is for.

## 8. Conditions the project proposes to accept

Commitments offered up front.
